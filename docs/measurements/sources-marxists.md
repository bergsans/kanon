# Marxists Internet Archive

[← back to README](../../README.md)

**Marxists Internet Archive** is the trickiest. The archive mixes three kinds
of text in the same catalog: works free by age, translations MIA itself
commissioned and released under Creative Commons, and texts they host without
owning the rights. The difference doesn't show in the address. Their Gramsci
page describes what happens when the line is crossed — Lawrence & Wishart
demanded in 2008 that Quintin Hoare's translations be withdrawn. So only
articles whose provenance line says where the translation came from are
indexed:

```
Source: L'Ordine Nuovo, 11 October 1919;
Translated: for the Marxists Internet Archive by Michael Carley.
```

Of Gramsci's 47 articles, 24 pass that check. Of Rosa Luxemburg's 74, 6 pass,
and of Alexandra Kollontai's 43, none pass — her texts are Progress Publishers
and Allison & Busby, not MIA's own. Both authors still stand in the list, as a
note on what's been tried.

That a Swedish question gets an answer in Swedish isn't the point. The point is
that the original outweighs the translation: a question about marriage as a
lie is answered by _The Father_ in Strindberg's own text, not by the 1912
English rendering of it.
